The improver from `docs/IMPROVE.md` is being built to `docs/PLAN_IMPROVE.md`: phases 1 (sign-in), 2 (the configuration's versions and the settings), 3 (the judge), 4 (the improver), 5 (the Improvement page) and 6 (the look of the dashboard, the live runs and the handover) are done: the build is complete. ## What's built

- `backend/`: FastAPI on SQLAlchemy Core. `backend/api.py` builds the app from the routers in `backend/routes/` (claims, runs, evaluations, usage, documents, and the library of wording, instructions and handlers). `backend/rules.py` holds the claim states, `backend/settings.py` the settings, `backend/versions.py` the configuration's versions and the recipe's, `backend/diffs.py` their diffs (with `difflib`), `backend/allowed.py` the models allowed, `backend/measures.py` an evaluation's measures, `backend/judge.py` the judge and `backend/grading.py` the grading of an evaluation, `backend/rounds.py` a round's record, `backend/verdict.py` our rule, `backend/spending.py` a round's spending and `backend/improver_view.py` what the improver reads, `backend/runs.py` the rules for a dispatch or an evaluation and the summary from the records, `backend/evaluations.py` the copies, `backend/scoring.py` the measures, `backend/leases.py` the claim leases, `backend/payout.py` the payout rules, `backend/turns.py` the limit of 8 requests inside at once, and `backend/agent_service.py` the line to the agent's service, and `backend/auth.py` who is signed in. `backend/db.py` holds the tables and the seed: Supabase's `undercover` schema when `DATABASE_URL` is set, otherwise SQLite (`undercover.db`, or the file in `UNDERCOVER_DB`). `npm run seed` (`uv run -m backend.seed`) starts again from `data/`, and stores the answer key from `data/evals/` in tables of its own.
  - Read: the queue (never an evaluation's copies), a claim in full, where a claim stands (`/standing`, a single query), the agent's steps, the policy wording, the sections of the wording and the claims manual and a search across them, and the handlers.
  - The configuration: the claim agent's instructions, model and reasoning effort, in versions (`config_versions`), and the improver's recipe (`recipe_versions`). Seeding makes version 1 of each, from `backend/instructions/claim.md` on `openai/gpt-6-luna` at `low` (or `MODEL_NAME` at `default`) and from `backend/instructions/recipe.md`, the lab's recipe. `GET /api/configuration/current` and `/api/configuration/versions/{n}` give the claim agent its instructions, model and effort, with no scores. The list of versions adds what each changed and its scores: the averages of the finished whole-set evaluations that rounds ran on it, per set. Each version and recipe has a diff against the one before.
  - Settings (`settings` and `setting_changes`): the approval limit, seeded from `data/company.json` ($800), and the business objectives, noise, tolerance, a round's spending limit and the agents at once, seeded from `data/settings.json`. `PUT /api/settings` records each change with who and when, and needs a handler who can sign off over the limit. The models allowed come from `data/models.json` (or only `MODEL_NAME`, at `default` and a price of 0) and can't be changed there.
  - The payout rules: `POST /api/claims/{id}/payout`, with the approval limit from the settings.
  - Sign-in: every handler action (a sign-off, "Run the agent", starting a dispatch or an evaluation) needs a handler's Supabase access token. `backend/auth.py` checks it against the project's public keys at `VITE_SUPABASE_URL` with PyJWT and finds the handler by the token's email. A missing, bad or expired token, or one for someone who isn't a handler, gets a 401. A sign-off also needs a handler who can sign off payouts over the limit (Dana Whitfield), or it gets a 403 that says why. The agent's own calls stay open until Week 3.
  - Claim states: a decline or a referral is `decided` at once. An approval up to the limit stays `in_progress` until it's paid. An approval over the limit is `awaiting_sign_off`. A sign-off records the signed-in handler and when, moves it to `in_progress` and doesn't pay; the backend then calls the agent's service, and the sign-off stands if the service can't be reached. The backend refuses a payment over the limit without a sign-off, and refuses any sign-off on an evaluation's copy.
  - Nothing happens twice: a repeat of the decision or the payment already recorded returns the claim as done, and a different one is refused. Steps have stable keys, and each model reply's usage is recorded once, by its id.
  - Failures: an error counts an attempt, forgets the thread and sends the claim back to start afresh; the second leaves it `needs_handler`. A claim with a decision can't fail: it stays in progress to be carried on.
  - Runs: `runs` holds dispatches and evaluations, with their kind, N, set, version (with its model and reasoning) and round, and a unique index keeps one running at a time. `POST /api/runs` (from the service) fixes the run's claims: for a dispatch, those in progress and then those waiting, oldest submitted first; for an evaluation, new copies such as `EV4-CLM-1001`. `POST /api/dispatches` and `POST /api/evaluations` ask the service to start one, passing on its 409, or a 503 when it isn't running. `GET /api/runs/current` is the latest dispatch, `GET /api/runs/running` whatever is running, and each run has its claims, model cost, time and, once ended, its summary. `GET /api/evaluations` and `/api/evaluations/{id}` add the measures.
  - The judge: Jev (`typesafe/jev-1.13`) through OpenRouter's Decisions endpoint, called only in `backend/judge.py`, with the lab's rubric and three questions, at the address in `JUDGE_URL` (read once; `https://openrouter.ai` by default), a 10-second timeout and no retries. As an evaluation finishes (`POST /api/runs/{id}/finish`), its claims are graded in parallel against the handlers' reasons and references, which seeding now stores: the grade is Jev's score from 0 to 3, the letter the most likely grade, and the two checks (same grounds, same section) are kept as probabilities in `judgements`. A claim the agent didn't decide gets an F without a call; one the judge couldn't grade is left out of the average. The scores add the average grade and the judge's cost, apart from the claim agent's. With `MODEL_NAME` set the judge isn't called.
  - Rounds of improvement (`rounds` and `round_events`): a round opens on its own copy of the settings and the latest recipe, and records where it has got to (first evaluations, the improver working, a change waiting, the evaluations, the verdict, a recipe waiting, finished or stopped), the proposal waiting with its diff, the version tried, the improver's reason, the held-out scores before and after, the verdict and why, each approval or rejection with who, when and why, and what it cost. `POST /api/rounds`, `/api/rounds/{id}/stop` and `/api/rounds/{id}/decision` need a handler who can sign off over the limit, and the first and last ask the agent's service, passing on a refusal that names what is running. The service records each step through open routes under `/api/rounds/{id}/`, which refuse anything once a round is stopped. Our rule (`backend/verdict.py`, the lab's `compare` and `keep_or_roll_back`) keeps the version tried when its held-out average meets the objectives, beats the noise on a measure and is no worse than the tolerance on any; a version with no grade, with evaluations that didn't all finish, or that the round couldn't afford, is rolled back, and a kept version becomes the current one. A round's spending covers the improver's calls, and in all its evaluations the claim agents' and the judge's; an evaluation's estimate is its claims times the highest cost per claim seen for its model.
  - Cost and time: `POST /api/claims/{id}/usage` (tokens and cost of a reply) and `POST /api/claims/{id}/work` (a spell of work), each with its run or none.
- `agent/`: the UnderCover Agent, in six parts. Each run carries its version, and the claim agent is built from that version's instructions, model and effort; outside a run it uses the current version.
  - The dispatcher (`agent/dispatcher.py`): `list_claims`, a `Send` per claim and `work_claim`, with `max_concurrency` set to N. A rate limit or a timeout is retried with growing waits; on the last of 4 attempts the claim is left in progress. A claim that failed and went back to the queue is tried once more by the same worker. Any other error in a worker leaves its claim for the next dispatch, so one claim never stops the run.
  - The claim agent (`agent/claim.py` and `agent/graph.py`, instructions in `backend/instructions/claim.md`). Its nodes are `agent`, `record`, `tools`, `sign_off`, `pay` and `close`. `claim.work(claim_id, run)` is the one door: it carries on a claim in progress or starts a waiting one, skips a claim another worker holds, and clears the thread of an evaluation's copy held for sign-off. It stops after 30 model calls.
  - Checkpoints (`agent/checkpoints.py`): a `PostgresSaver` for each run of the graph, on one pool of at most 4 connections that waits up to 120 seconds; in memory without `DATABASE_URL`, which only the tests rely on.
  - The service (`agent/service.py`), on port 8002: start a dispatch, an evaluation or a round, approve or reject a round's proposal, start a claim, resume a signed-off claim. One piece of work at a time, and a refusal names what is running, such as "Dispatch 7 is running"; a round's evaluations are part of its own work, and while a round waits for a handler, dispatches and evaluations can run. When it starts, it marks a run or a round left working as stopped (a round waiting for a handler carries on later) and pays anything signed off while it was away.
  - The improver (`agent/improver.py`, with the round's work in `agent/rounds.py`): a LangChain `create_agent` on GPT-6 Luna at medium effort, with the lab's four tools (`read_results`, `propose_change`, `run_evaluation`, `propose_recipe`), which reach the backend only through `agent/improver_client.py`. Its instructions (`backend/instructions/improver.md`) are written as goals around the round's recipe. `HumanInTheLoopMiddleware` pauses on each proposal (one outside the models allowed goes straight back to it), `ModelCallLimitMiddleware` caps it at 20 model calls a round, and its own middleware turns off parallel tool calls, records each reply's cost with the round and stops calling the model once the round is stopped. Each round has a thread of its own in Postgres. A round first runs the current version's missing whole-set evaluations (one on the evaluation set, two on the held-out set), then the improver until it pauses or finishes; `run_evaluation` runs the version tried the same way, through the dispatcher in a fresh context, and asks the backend for the verdict. The dispatcher starts no claim in a round's evaluation once the round is stopped or has spent its limit, and such an evaluation shows as stopped.
  - The model (`agent/model.py`): the version's model and effort (`default` sets none), or `MODEL_NAME` with none. Tracing (`agent/tracing.py`): each claim's work is one Langfuse session, named by its thread and tagged with its run, such as `dispatch-2`, and with its round, such as `round-3`. The improver's work in a round is one session, named after the round's thread.
- `dashboard/`: React in UnderCover's brand, light and dark.
  - Every page waits for a handler to sign in with their email and password (`dashboard/src/auth.ts` is the only module that reaches Supabase; there's no sign-up). The header shows who is signed in, with "Sign out", and every action sends the session's access token.
  - `#/`: the queue, "Start a dispatch" with "Claim agents at once" (1 to 25, 3 to start with), and the latest dispatch's claims, cost, time and summary. While a dispatch or evaluation runs, the start is disabled.
  - `#/claims/CLM-1001`: a claim in full, with its decision, what the agent read, its failed attempts, its steps as they arrive, "Run the agent" and sign-off as the handler signed in. Marcus Oyelaran and Priya Raman see why they can't sign off a payout over the limit. An evaluation's copy says so and has no sign-off form.
  - `#/live`: the live view, full screen: the latest dispatch as a board of cards, the ones at work first, each with the agent's latest step and stamped as it ends (an approval once it's paid), with the numbers, cost and time across the top and the summary from the records at the end. Esc leaves.
  - `#/scoreboard` and `#/scoreboard/4`: start an evaluation, the evaluations with their measures (the grade and the judge's cost among them), and each evaluation's claims beside the handlers' answers, the misses marked, each with its grade and the two checks as yes or no.
  - `#/configuration` (where `#/instructions` now leads): the current version, every version with what it changed, who proposed and approved it and its held-out scores, and any version's changes as a diff in color.
  - `#/improvement`: start or stop a round (after a confirmation), where the open round has got to as it happens (the first evaluations, the improver at work, a change waiting, the evaluations, the verdict, a recipe waiting, finished), the proposal waiting with the instructions or the recipe as a diff and the model and effort before and after, approve after a confirmation or reject with a reason, the rounds so far newest first with the version tried, the held-out scores before and after, the verdict and why, who approved or rejected what, and the cost, a chart in SVG of each version's held-out accuracy and grade, and the recipe's versions with the same diff view. Marcus Oyelaran and Priya Raman see it all, with why they can't act.
  - `#/settings`: the settings, editable by Dana Whitfield after a confirmation in the dashboard's own dialog and read only for the others, with why; the changes, the models allowed, and the appearance (light, dark or as the computer), which the browser remembers.
- `e2e/`: Playwright tests, each signed in through a stand-in for Supabase Auth on port 8004 (`e2e/auth-standin.ts`, with ES256 keys of its own), with a stand-in for the judge on port 8005 (`e2e/judge-standin.ts`), of signing in, the queue, a claim, the sign-off, the instructions, starting a dispatch and an evaluation (the stand-in on port 8003 records each request's body), the run panel's summary, one run at a time, the live board and the scoreboard. They run on their own ports with a fresh SQLite database and play the agent's part through the API.

## How to run it

```
uv sync
npm install
npm run seed
npm run dev
```

Then open http://localhost:5173. The backend is on port 8000 and the agent's service on port 8002. `.env` needs `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`, `DATABASE_URL`, the Langfuse keys, and `VITE_SUPABASE_URL` and `VITE_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY` for sign-in. Handlers sign in with the password set for them in Supabase Auth (`HANDLER_PASSWORD`). After a change to the schema, run `npm run seed`.

- One claim: "Run the agent" on the claim's page, or `uv run -m agent CLM-1001`.
- The whole queue: "Start a dispatch" on the queue page.
- An evaluation: the Scoreboard page.
- `npm run check` runs every check, with no need for `.env`.
- To stop `npm run dev` on Windows, stop it as a whole (Ctrl+C, or `taskkill /T` on its process). Killing only the process on port 8000 leaves uvicorn's reload worker running on the old code, still holding its 4 connections.

## The last live run

On 4 and 5 November 2026, on GPT-6 Luna, after one `npm run seed`, with `npm run dev` running. Screenshots are in `docs/screenshots/improve-*.png`. - Round 1, signed in as Dana Whitfield, from the Improvement page. Its three evaluations ran, and our rule rolled it back: held-out accuracy went from 80% to 84% and the grade from 1.20 to 1.29, both inside the noise. The improver's new recipe was approved. Each stage showed on the page as it happened. The round cost $0.152 of its $0.50 limit, in 22 minutes.

- Version 2 scored 92% on the evaluation set against version 1's 84%, but only 84% on the held-out set: the gain on the claims the improver studied didn't carry over, which is what the held-out set is for.
- Version 1's two held-out runs differed by 8 points of accuracy (76% and 84%; noise 10, tolerance 10), 0.07 of grade (1.16 and 1.23; noise 0.15), 3% of cost per claim ($0.00094 and $0.00097; noise 15%) and 27% of time per claim (132.6 and 104.5 seconds; noise 15%, tolerance 25%). With no change at all, time per claim moved by more than its tolerance, as decision 8 of the plan warned: a version can be rolled back for time alone. Widen the time tolerance on the Settings page if that happens.

Two problems were found and fixed. On Windows, uvicorn's reload hung twice after a change to `backend/`, leaving the old process serving the old code, so `npm run dev` was stopped as a whole and started again each time. And with Supabase a round trip away, the scoreboard and the versions took 9 to 11 seconds to load, because each ended evaluation's measures were worked out again on every request. An ended evaluation never changes, so `backend/measures.py` now keeps its measures once worked out: 0.3 and 1 second.
