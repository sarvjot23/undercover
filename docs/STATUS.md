A dispatcher with no model works the queue with up to 25 claim agents at once, and the Scoreboard evaluates the agent against our handlers' decisions.

## What's built

- `backend/`: FastAPI on SQLAlchemy Core. `backend/api.py` builds the app from the routers in `backend/routes/` (claims, runs, evaluations, usage, documents, and the library of wording, instructions and handlers). `backend/rules.py` holds the claim states and the approval limit, `backend/runs.py` the rules for a dispatch or an evaluation and the summary from the records, `backend/evaluations.py` the copies, `backend/scoring.py` the measures, `backend/leases.py` the claim leases, `backend/payout.py` the payout rules, `backend/turns.py` the limit of 8 requests inside at once, and `backend/agent_service.py` the line to the agent's service, and `backend/auth.py` who is signed in. `backend/db.py` holds the tables and the seed: Supabase's `undercover` schema when `DATABASE_URL` is set, otherwise SQLite (`undercover.db`, or the file in `UNDERCOVER_DB`). `npm run seed` (`uv run -m backend.seed`) starts again from `data/`, and stores the answer key from `data/evals/` in tables of its own.
  - Read: the queue (never an evaluation's copies), a claim in full, where a claim stands (`/standing`, a single query), the agent's steps, the policy wording, the sections of the wording and the claims manual and a search across them, the claim agent's instructions (`backend/instructions/claim.md`) and the handlers.
  - The payout rules: `POST /api/claims/{id}/payout`, with the limit from `payout_approval_limit` in `data/company.json` ($800).
  - Sign-in: every handler action (a sign-off, "Run the agent", starting a dispatch or an evaluation) needs a handler's Supabase access token. `backend/auth.py` checks it against the project's public keys at `VITE_SUPABASE_URL` with PyJWT and finds the handler by the token's email. A missing, bad or expired token, or one for someone who isn't a handler, gets a 401. A sign-off also needs a handler who can sign off payouts over the limit (Dana Whitfield), or it gets a 403 that says why. The agent's own calls stay open until Week 3.
  - Claim states: a decline or a referral is `decided` at once. An approval up to the limit stays `in_progress` until it's paid. An approval over the limit is `awaiting_sign_off`. A sign-off records the signed-in handler and when, moves it to `in_progress` and doesn't pay; the backend then calls the agent's service, and the sign-off stands if the service can't be reached. The backend refuses a payment over the limit without a sign-off, and refuses any sign-off on an evaluation's copy.
  - Nothing happens twice: a repeat of the decision or the payment already recorded returns the claim as done, and a different one is refused. Steps have stable keys, and each model reply's usage is recorded once, by its id.
  - Failures: an error counts an attempt, forgets the thread and sends the claim back to start afresh; the second leaves it `needs_handler`. A claim with a decision can't fail: it stays in progress to be carried on.
  - Runs: `runs` holds dispatches and evaluations, with their kind, N, set, model and reasoning, and a unique index keeps one running at a time. `POST /api/runs` (from the service) fixes the run's claims: for a dispatch, those in progress and then those waiting, oldest submitted first; for an evaluation, new copies such as `EV4-CLM-1001`. `POST /api/dispatches` and `POST /api/evaluations` ask the service to start one, passing on its 409, or a 503 when it isn't running. `GET /api/runs/current` is the latest dispatch, `GET /api/runs/running` whatever is running, and each run has its claims, model cost, time and, once ended, its summary. `GET /api/evaluations` and `/api/evaluations/{id}` add the measures.
  - Cost and time: `POST /api/claims/{id}/usage` (tokens and cost of a reply) and `POST /api/claims/{id}/work` (a spell of work), each with its run or none.
- `agent/`: the UnderCover Agent, in five parts.
  - The dispatcher (`agent/dispatcher.py`): `list_claims`, a `Send` per claim and `work_claim`, with `max_concurrency` set to N. A rate limit or a timeout is retried with growing waits; on the last of 4 attempts the claim is left in progress. A claim that failed and went back to the queue is tried once more by the same worker. Any other error in a worker leaves its claim for the next dispatch, so one claim never stops the run.
  - The claim agent (`agent/claim.py` and `agent/graph.py`, instructions in `backend/instructions/claim.md`). Its nodes are `agent`, `record`, `tools`, `sign_off`, `pay` and `close`. `claim.work(claim_id, run)` is the one door: it carries on a claim in progress or starts a waiting one, skips a claim another worker holds, and clears the thread of an evaluation's copy held for sign-off. It stops after 30 model calls.
  - Checkpoints (`agent/checkpoints.py`): a `PostgresSaver` for each run of the graph, on one pool of at most 4 connections that waits up to 120 seconds; in memory without `DATABASE_URL`, which only the tests rely on.
  - The service (`agent/service.py`), on port 8002: start a dispatch or an evaluation (one at a time), start a claim, resume a signed-off claim. When it starts, it marks a run left running as stopped and pays anything signed off while it was away.
  - The model (`agent/model.py`): `openai/gpt-6-luna` with low effort, or `MODEL_NAME` with none. Tracing (`agent/tracing.py`): each claim's work is one Langfuse session, named by its thread and tagged with its run, such as `dispatch-2`.
- `dashboard/`: React in UnderCover's brand, light and dark.
  - Every page waits for a handler to sign in with their email and password (`dashboard/src/auth.ts` is the only module that reaches Supabase; there's no sign-up). The header shows who is signed in, with "Sign out", and every action sends the session's access token.
  - `#/`: the queue, "Start a dispatch" with "Claim agents at once" (1 to 25, 3 to start with), and the latest dispatch's claims, cost, time and summary. While a dispatch or evaluation runs, the start is disabled.
  - `#/claims/CLM-1001`: a claim in full, with its decision, what the agent read, its failed attempts, its steps as they arrive, "Run the agent" and sign-off as the handler signed in. Marcus Oyelaran and Priya Raman see why they can't sign off a payout over the limit. An evaluation's copy says so and has no sign-off form.
  - `#/live`: the live view, full screen: the latest dispatch as a board of cards, the ones at work first, each with the agent's latest step and stamped as it ends (an approval once it's paid), with the numbers, cost and time across the top and the summary from the records at the end. Esc leaves.
  - `#/scoreboard` and `#/scoreboard/4`: start an evaluation, the evaluations with their measures, and each evaluation's claims beside the handlers' answers, the misses marked.
  - `#/instructions`: the claim agent's instructions, read only.
- `e2e/`: Playwright tests, each signed in through a stand-in for Supabase Auth on port 8004 (`e2e/auth-standin.ts`, with ES256 keys of its own), of signing in, the queue, a claim, the sign-off, the instructions, starting a dispatch and an evaluation (the stand-in on port 8003 records each request's body), the run panel's summary, one run at a time, the live board and the scoreboard. They run on their own ports with a fresh SQLite database and play the agent's part through the API.

## How to run it

```
uv sync
npm install
npm run seed
npm run dev
```

Then open http://localhost:5173. The backend is on port 8000 and the agent's service on port 8002. `.env` needs `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`, `DATABASE_URL`, the Langfuse keys, and `VITE_SUPABASE_URL` and `VITE_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY` for sign-in. Handlers sign in with the password set for them in Supabase Auth (`HANDLER_PASSWORD`). After a change to the schema, run `npm run seed`.

- One claim: "Run the agent" on the claim's page, or `uv run -m agent CLM-1001`.
- The whole queue: "Start a dispatch" on the queue page.
- An evaluation: the Scoreboard page.
- `npm run check` runs every check, with no need for `.env`.
- To stop `npm run dev` on Windows, stop it as a whole (Ctrl+C, or `taskkill /T` on its process). Killing only the process on port 8000 leaves uvicorn's reload worker running on the old code, still holding its 4 connections.

## The last live run

On 4 November 2026, on GPT-6 Luna with low effort, after `npm run seed`, with `npm run dev` running. The browser was driven with Playwright's library from a script, because the Playwright MCP server didn't connect that day. Screenshots are in `docs/screenshots/swarm-*.png`.

- A dispatch at N=3 was stopped partway by killing the process on port 8002 (on Windows the service is one process). It had decided 5 claims and left 3 in progress. When `npm run dev` started again, the service marked it stopped.
- A dispatch at N=25 carried on those 3 on their own threads, with no new attempt, one decision each and no repeated steps, and started the other 17 within 3.1 seconds of each other: 20 at once. It took 155 seconds, against 2,123 seconds of agent time on its claims, and cost $0.0231. Its summary: 12 approved, 4 declined, 4 referred, with CLM-1003 ($3,750), CLM-1021 and CLM-1009 waiting for sign-off.
- Claims took longer each at N=25: 71 to 94 seconds for claims worked from the start in one attempt, against 24 to 59 at N=3. The model wasn't slower (a median of 1.9 seconds a call against 1.8, from Langfuse). The time goes to the backend and the checkpointer: 25 agents share 4 connections each, and that day a round trip to Supabase took 0.3 to 0.8 seconds.
- 14 claims of that dispatch failed once, and were worked again in the same dispatch and decided. The failures were caused by the test, not the build: an orphaned backend worker from an earlier restart was still on port 8000, and stopping it reset their requests.
- Signed off on the dashboard, CLM-1009 was paid 7.0 seconds after the click and CLM-1021 5.5 seconds after, against the five seconds asked for. Picking a signed-off claim back up and paying it makes about eight round trips to Supabase in turn, which took 0.3 to 0.8 seconds each that day.
- An evaluation of the whole evaluation set at N=25 took 135 seconds with no failed attempts: decision accuracy and accuracy 84%, cost of mistakes $553, straight through 88%, $0.0009 a claim. The held-out set's five `tiny` claims at N=5: 100%, 100%, $0, 80%. The queue's counts were the same before and after. An earlier evaluation, stuck by the problem below and stopped, shows as stopped with no measures.
- During the runs, `pg_stat_activity` showed 8 connections for user postgres (7 through the pooler and Supabase's own `pg_net`), once the orphaned worker was gone.
- Through Langfuse's v2 observations API, the tag `dispatch-2` finds that dispatch's claims together: 65 traces in 65 sessions. Run ids start again after a reseed, so a tag is unique only within one seed. Langfuse's older traces API is closed to this account and will be removed on 16 November 2026.

Two problems were found and fixed during the runs. A first N=25 dispatch failed 19 claims and then stopped, because the agent's calls to the backend gave up after 30 seconds while the 4 connections were busy, and the error left the worker. Requests now wait up to 120 seconds for a connection, the agent waits 150, and the worker leaves any unexpected error's claim for the next dispatch. Then an evaluation at N=25 stalled for minutes: FastAPI checks out a connection in one worker thread and runs the handler in another, so every thread could wait for a connection while the requests holding them waited for a thread. `backend/turns.py` now keeps at most 8 requests inside at once, and the rest wait on the event loop.
